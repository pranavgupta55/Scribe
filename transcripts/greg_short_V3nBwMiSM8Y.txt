At the time, those felt like world-ending colossal mistakes, catastrophic mistakes. Looking back, they were some of the cheapest lessons I could have ever bought. The problem is that most people, after a few mistakes, become obsessed with certainty. They overthink every decision. They wait for the perfect timing and try to eliminate every possible risk before their next move. But that's where progress dies. That's where entrepreneurship dies. That's where you stop being a great founder. Perfection feels intelligent. It feels safe. But in reality, making a decision that is late because you are waiting for perfection and therefore move slowly is often far more dangerous than making fast decision and improving at real time.
