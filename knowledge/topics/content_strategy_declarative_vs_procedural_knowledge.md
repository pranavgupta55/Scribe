# Content Strategy: Declarative vs. Procedural Knowledge

> Teach how it works conceptually (declarative), create belief shifts (old model vs. new model), then sell implementation (procedural).

## Claims
- Separate secrets (information) from implementation (execution). Give away all the secrets and sell the implementation; 99% of people won't buy but will judge you on the quality of free content. — [watch_this_to_get_ahead_in_2026_spXH1kJ6q-Q.txt]
- Declarative knowledge teaches about something (concepts, frameworks, why things work); procedural knowledge teaches how to do something. Use declarative knowledge to sell. — [watch_this_to_get_ahead_in_2026_spXH1kJ6q-Q.txt]
- Example of declarative: 'Crossing from $2M to $5M revenue raises your valuation multiple from 3x to 7x, turning a $4M business into a $35M business.' This builds belief without teaching a how-to. — [watch_this_to_get_ahead_in_2026_spXH1kJ6q-Q.txt]
- Procedural knowledge (tactics) requires people to execute independently to extract value, delaying purchase; declarative knowledge creates immediate value just by exposure. — [watch_this_to_get_ahead_in_2026_spXH1kJ6q-Q.txt]
- Teach how it works conceptually (declarative), create belief shifts (old model vs. new model), then sell implementation (procedural). — [watch_this_to_get_ahead_in_2026_spXH1kJ6q-Q.txt]
