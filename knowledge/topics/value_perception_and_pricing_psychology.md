# Value Perception and Pricing Psychology

> Rich customers shop price high-to-low (looking at premium first) because they want better value; poor customers shop low-to-high to minimize cost

## Claims
- Rich customers shop price high-to-low (looking at premium first) because they want better value; poor customers shop low-to-high to minimize cost — [why_you_aren_t_making_as_much_money_as_you_want_ZuJryiwxjDw.txt]
- Wealthy customers evaluate ROI and value (expensive Lamborghini at $20K is 'cheap'); poor customers only see price (expensive car at $20K is 'expensive') — [why_you_aren_t_making_as_much_money_as_you_want_ZuJryiwxjDw.txt]
- The only thing worse than offering $1,000 thing to $100-budget person (lose $100) is offering $100 thing to $1,000-budget person (lose $900 in opportunity profit) — [why_you_aren_t_making_as_much_money_as_you_want_ZuJryiwxjDw.txt]
- Pricing signals to marketplace: high price = high value in buyers' minds; advanced service business owner is one with highest pricing relative to comparable services — [why_you_aren_t_making_as_much_money_as_you_want_ZuJryiwxjDw.txt]
- Pricing psychology tactic: before revealing price, say 'it's super expensive' to anchor prospect emotionally; rich person mentally prepares for high number; poor person braces for shock — [why_you_aren_t_making_as_much_money_as_you_want_ZuJryiwxjDw.txt]
- Advanced service business owner signals marketplace through pricing; they continue raising price until supply-demand equilibrium, then raise again as reputation improves—never stops climbing — [why_you_aren_t_making_as_much_money_as_you_want_ZuJryiwxjDw.txt]
